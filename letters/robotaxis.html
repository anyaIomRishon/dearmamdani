Transportation, Safety · November 12, 2026

You'll save the horses. What about the drivers?

Dear Mayor Mamdani,

You've decided that horse carriages are unsafe, outdated and must end, and that a 150-year-old tradition is no excuse for keeping animals in dangerous work. I agree. So why doesn't the same logic apply to people?

This summer, four New York yellow cab drivers died on the job. Richard Coulibaly, Khalifa Siwa and Jagjit Singh died alone in their cabs: from a weak heart during a July heat wave, from complications of diabetes, from a suspected heart attack. Their fellow drivers say they kept working because they couldn't afford to stop. The danger doesn't stop at the driver's seat. Taxis and for-hire cars were involved in more than 10,600 crashes in 2024, about one every 49 minutes. Last year 205 people died on our streets, 111 of them pedestrians. Almost every one of those crashes had a human being behind the wheel.

Meanwhile, a safer option exists, and you've shut it out. Across more than 270 million driverless miles, Waymo was involved in 82% fewer injury crashes and 95% fewer serious-injury-or-worse crashes than human drivers in the same cities. In Los Angeles, it has had zero serious-injury crashes in 67 million miles. New York let even its tiny test, eight cars with safety drivers on board, expire in March, and you have not renewed it.

So here is the hypocrisy: when horses collapse, it's a crisis that ends an industry. When people die, it's a reason to protect the industry exactly as it is. You've promised carriage workers a just transition. Give drivers the same: health coverage now, real retraining, and a path out of a job that is wearing them down, instead of a city government that keeps them in it.

If the horses deserve better, so do the people. Renew Waymo's permit, back state legislation for safe autonomous service, and stop treating a dangerous job as something worth preserving.

Anya Levitov
New York, NY
