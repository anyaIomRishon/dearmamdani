Our agenda

What we're asking City Hall for

A city where people can afford to live, get to work safely and send their kids to good schools. These are the changes we're pushing for. Add your name to all of them, or sign the letters on the issues you care about most.

  1. Let robotaxis serve New York, safely

    Self-driving taxis are carrying riders in other American cities. Allow them here under clear safety rules, and judge them on the crash data.

  2. Keep the specialized high schools and the SHSAT

    Admission by an open, objective test gives every child in every borough the same shot at Stuyvesant, Bronx Science and Brooklyn Tech.

  3. Restore Bloomberg’s 421-a tax abatement

    Under Mayor Bloomberg, the 421-a tax abatement, which cut property taxes on new apartment buildings for up to 25 years, came with a condition in the city’s most prestigious neighborhoods: set aside 20% of the apartments as affordable. Over the life of the program, about 5,800 affordable apartments were built inside market-rate buildings this way. At 40 Riverside Boulevard on the Upper West Side, about 88,000 people applied for 55 affordable apartments. At Sky on West 42nd Street, about 91,000 households applied for 235.

    Many publications lamented the separate entrances to the condo and rental portions and shamefully claimed these projects were separating rich and poor. In fact, they allowed thousands of New Yorkers to move to premium neighborhoods and send their kids to better schools. It is the best way to create an integrated city, where lower-income families are not segregated in 100% affordable housing projects and have a chance to connect with their more accomplished and fortunate neighbors and build social capital. It is especially valuable for children.

    The replacement, 485-x, adds wage rules that start at 100 apartments, so builders now stop at 99. This spring, only 9 of 172 proposed buildings were that large. In two and a half years, builders have registered plans for only 326 affordable apartments in Manhattan. Restore the Bloomberg-era rules, with market-rate apartments free of rent regulation, and let New York build big again.
